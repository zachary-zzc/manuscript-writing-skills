### Introduction

For full research-manuscript drafts, write **4–5 substantial paragraphs totalling more than 800 words**, unless a later explicit author instruction changes this requirement. Use the available evidence to develop the scientific context, relevant prior findings, precise unresolved question and study rationale in a connected argument. Give each paragraph enough reasoning and literature support to perform its distinct role; do not meet the length by repeating the gap, adding generic background or previewing every result. If a binding submission format conflicts, make the conflict explicit rather than silently shortening the working draft. Verify both total words and paragraph count.

Open with scientific activity and a precise unresolved problem relevant to the study; broader background should lead directly to its contribution. Give each paragraph a distinct argumentative function. Support drawbacks with relevant primary evidence and named examples that actually demonstrate them. Trace the argument from a documented characteristic of an existing resource or method to its supported scientific consequence and the requirement relevant to the present study. Differences in source, scope or processing do not by themselves establish error, contamination or analytical bias. Do not assert that existing systems cannot perform a task when only a particular protocol or interface was evaluated.

State what remains unknown, disputed, unavailable or insufficiently established and why it matters for the actual contribution. The absence of the author's particular method is not, by itself, a scientific gap. When prior evidence conflicts, represent both positions fairly and identify the question that can distinguish them; do not manufacture a dispute to motivate the paper.

Explain why knowledge, data or methodology matters to the research question, bringing component motivations into one argument. Before moving from a scientific subject to a data-generation technology, dataset or computational method, establish how it serves the question being developed. Introduce a proposed solution after the need it addresses is clear; a related topic or shared term is not sufficient preparation. Avoid generic download difficulties, narrow unexplained examples, exaggerated prior-work weaknesses and unsupported claims that one bottleneck has replaced another. Do not prematurely solve every problem in its own paragraph or repeat the same motivation across neighbours.

Finish with a proportionate contribution and evaluation overview linked to the motivating problems. Select decisive outcomes without rehearsing all Results. Under the author's convention, omit figure and table callouts from the Introduction unless explicitly requested otherwise.

For full drafting or substantive restructuring, check motivation and promises in both directions: identify where the Introduction motivates each central Results question, and which Results evidence answers each question the Introduction promises to address. Several questions may share evidence, and one question may require several subsections; no one-to-one correspondence is required. Keep these links in the existing storyline record. Repair an unmotivated central result or narrow an unanswered promise without inventing evidence. A bounded edit checks only the affected links.

**Gate:** each paragraph advances the problem–contribution argument and prepares readers for an actual evaluation. The section is not a component inventory.
