### Captions and tables

Do not create an artificial manuscript-body section solely to collect common figure definitions. Put general variable definitions and procedures in Methods, and include the sample, denominator, units and encoding details needed to interpret each figure in its own caption. Repeat a short essential definition where this makes a caption self-contained; retain a shared legend note only when the verified publication format requires or clearly supports it.

Legends must explain every panel and necessary line, symbol, colour and reference mark. Identify conditions, data, units, aggregation/sample unit, centre/spread, error bars and statistical notation where present. Main and SI legends receive equal care. Avoid version labels, unexplained study shorthand and colon-stacked miniheadings. Update artwork, labels, legend and narrative together.

Apply the shared [direct-prose guidance](../../manuscript-workflow/references/writing-style.md#write-direct-academic-prose) and [sentence and reader-paraphrase checks](../../manuscript-workflow/references/paragraph-review.md#3-sentence-continuity) to the affected caption or table description. Keep labels concise without obscuring the measured quantity or relationship; this scoped check does not require a full manuscript audit.

Tables should answer a consequential quantitative or substantive comparative question. Avoid checklist filler, unsupported capability rankings and arbitrary table quotas. Include units, direction where useful, consistent precision, denominators and material protocol differences. Use restrained, readable structure and concise labels. Do not repeat all table values in prose or duplicate content in the legend.

**Gate:** each display and its legend are independently interpretable, faithful to source evidence and consistent with the narrative.
