### Results overview and architecture

Make Results self-contained. Explain what was evaluated and why; do not refer vaguely to requirements “above.” Prefer conclusive subsection headings that state supported discoveries. Avoid repeating the system name in every heading, deeply nested microsections, audit-log organisation and unsupported standalone claims.

**Gate:** headings and opening sentences communicate the accepted scientific argument in order. Each heading matches its actual contents, and every subsection has enough evidence for its claimed role.

### Results evidence and synthesis

For full research manuscripts, develop each main-text Results subsection as a complete scientific result aspect, not a list of analysis outputs. Each subsection must contain **at least 600 words of substantive prose**, excluding its heading, captions, tables and reference list. Build a connected account of the question, the evidence and comparisons, meaningful variation or sensitivity, and the supported interpretation. Give paragraphs distinct roles that together complete the result aspect; do not meet the minimum through repeated numerical summaries, generic background, duplicated Methods, unsupported mechanisms or invented observations. Count and report the words in every Results subsection during verification. If evidence cannot support the requested depth, reorganise genuinely related findings where scientifically justified or report the specific evidence gap; never fabricate material or silently waive the requirement. A later explicit author instruction may override this minimum; identify a conflict with a binding venue limit instead of silently shortening the subsections.

Apply these general rules across disciplines. Evidence may consist of measurements, qualitative observations, documentary sources, derivations or proofs. Do not require numerical scores, experiments or biological discoveries in work whose actual contribution has a different form. For measured/scored outcomes, predictive methods, biological claims, autonomous agents or human intervention/assessment, read only the matching prerequisites in [results-conditions.md](results-conditions.md).

Begin each subsection with a natural account of its scientific question, actual approach and result. Explain how each investigation contributes to that question. When a subsection addresses several questions or comparisons, distinguish them in the opening and organise the evidence accordingly. Introduce additional tasks through their scientific relevance rather than presenting isolated evaluations. Name actual comparison methods with technically precise terms.

Open principal Results paragraphs with the supported finding and identify the comparator when relevant. Establish the task naturally, provide concrete numerical or other appropriate evidence, compare the analytical scope relevant to the finding, and explain its scientific meaning. Avoid analysis inventories. Report parity, trade-offs and worse outcomes honestly rather than forcing an advantage. Comparative wording must identify the supported dimension and its scientific meaning. Apply this finding-first approach to result summaries in the Introduction and Abstract as well as the Results. Do not present dataset size, cluster count, roadmap depth or analytical breadth as a scientific accomplishment on their own; explain what the added data or analyses established, resolved or made possible, and supply the evidence. Retain counts that substantiate a relevant scale, coverage or resource contribution, with their role made explicit, and keep necessary procedural counts in Methods or supporting material. When comparing systems, trace the specific analytical extension to its supported finding or interpretation, distinguish differences in scope from differences in accuracy, and do not infer that more data or deeper roadmaps caused a better result without evidence.

In Results, state observed cases, relationships and measurements directly as findings; do not introduce them with “for example.” Retain specific cases when they substantiate the result, identify their scope and supporting evidence, and avoid generalising from selected cases to an unmeasured population.

Develop the evidence beyond a brief finding summary. Explain enough of the completed reasoning or scientific workflow to show how the conclusion follows from the inputs and observations, selecting stages relevant to the claim rather than listing tools or tasks. Include meaningful variation, sensitivity or incompleteness when it changes the conclusion. Distinguish reconstruction of published findings, newly performed analyses and new hypotheses, and credit the original work. Expand from retained evidence when requested; do not fill gaps with repetition or conjecture. Keep one scientific purpose per paragraph. Avoid fragmenting a Results subsection into many short paragraphs that isolate a finding from its evidence, comparison or interpretation. Develop substantial, cohesive paragraphs; combine closely related observations and necessary qualifications when they serve the same scientific point, rewriting topic sentences and transitions rather than merely deleting paragraph breaks. Preserve separate paragraphs for genuinely different questions or analytical units. Audit every short paragraph for an independent argumentative role and record paragraph counts and word-length distributions alongside subsection totals; do not invent a universal per-paragraph word minimum.

Keep interpretations at the level and scope actually studied. State which relationship, process, property, explanation or candidate the evidence supports and why it matters in the discipline. Generic claims of broader or deeper interpretation do not supply the scientific conclusion. Use the discipline's distinction between observation, inference and established knowledge; the biological examples and predictive-evaluation rules in the conditional reference do not apply to unrelated work.

Keep a local interpretation in Results when it resolves the question just examined, its support is visible in the subsection, and relevant uncertainty or credible alternatives are clear. If explaining it requires a broader synthesis of theory, literature or several findings, retain the supported local inference here and develop that synthesis in Discussion. This is a distinction of argumentative function, not a ban on interpretation in Results.

Preserve comparison and condition-specific credibility. Identify what was compared and attribute each finding to the actual object, method, population or condition evaluated. Name relevant historical versions and configurations. Distinguish an analysis absent from a comparator's evaluated output from a capability the comparator lacks. Shared objectives or instructions do not establish matched inputs, equal validation or an isolated causal effect. When results pool or unite outputs from several conditions, identify the combination rule and do not attribute the combined result to one condition. Apply the specific quantitative, model, agent and human-role distinctions only when their prerequisites are met.

Where figures or tables are present, cite the relevant display beside the result, workflow coverage or structural interpretation it supports. A citation beside a headline score does not automatically support an earlier claim about analytical completeness. Follow the scientific argument rather than panel order. For figures showing structure at several scales, explain the relationship among those scales. Include individual entities or edges only when they establish the paragraph's scientific point, and do not imply a hierarchy the analysis has not established.

Keep operational trivia and detailed procedural defence out unless they affect inference. Integrate scientific meaning with its supporting results. Do not append a standalone closing paragraph that merely repeats findings or scores; retain a closing synthesis when it adds a necessary scientific inference.

**Gate:** each conclusion has appropriate evidence in the correct scope and condition. The subsection adds a distinct finding, necessary support or a material boundary without introducing irrelevant study types, actors or instruments.

### Place evidence by its role

When drafting or restructuring Results, refine the existing H4 allocation record rather than creating another ledger. Identify whether an item supplies a central finding, necessary support, an interpretation-changing qualification, robustness, meaningful heterogeneity, provenance, an alternative analysis or a failure boundary. These are functions, not mandatory study components or exclusive labels. Record only the distinctions needed to explain its effect on the conclusion, destination and supporting pointer; a bounded edit checks its affected items.

Keep central findings, indispensable support and conclusion-changing evidence visible in the main text. A robustness or subgroup analysis belongs there when it resolves a central alternative, establishes a necessary condition or changes where the conclusion holds; a secondary check that adds no such inference can live in SI. Classify by scientific role, never by whether the outcome is favourable. Put procedural provenance in Methods or supporting material and display-reading definitions in captions. Preserve mandatory reporting, reproducibility and integrity content in its required location. Allocation does not waive the subsection length and evidence-depth requirements above.

For quantitative findings, retain the estimate and uncertainty or inferential information needed to judge the claim. Choose a clear location for the complete numerical report and avoid repeating every effect size, interval and test in both narrative and caption. Each caption must still be independently interpretable and satisfy the actual format. Keep the full relevant analysis family traceable, including secondary or unfavourable analyses, and note reporting locations in the same record when the choice matters. Do not select the most favourable statistic or use a pointer to conceal a material discrepancy.

## Results production loop

Use H4's record to test each subsection: does it advance/support/materially bound the central claim, have concrete evidence, and occupy the right place in the sequence? Move peripheral material to SI or integrate it where appropriate. Preserve important null results and qualifications; storyline emphasis must not suppress evidence. Do not force one drawback, module and subsection into a one-to-one mapping.

For a substantive subsection change, apply the [distinct-contribution test](../../manuscript-storyline/references/planning.md#existing-argument-and-handoff) to the affected subsection and its neighbours. Reuse the accepted architecture; a local wording change does not reopen H4.

Prepare the subsection and verified display/legend, state its story role and consequential open question, then follow H7's mode-specific review. Revise the specified material while preserving accepted decisions; apply when authorized, synchronize dependent passages and inspect affected output. Record the completed baseline and proceed. Concept approval and draft approval differ. Where draft-first review applies, wait before applying dependent prose. An existing implementation instruction or waiver governs its scope; routine authorized fixes need no repeated permission.
