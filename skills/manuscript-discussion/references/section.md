### Discussion and ending

Plan by scientific functions: central advance, synthesis across findings, relation to prior work, meaningful limitations and scientific implications. These functions are not mandatory paragraphs or a fixed order. Allocate them to the actual argument and evidence type; do not impose a study type, participant role or paragraph count. Anchor the opening in the advance motivated by the Introduction and supported by Results, giving each central strand appropriate weight without forcing equal space or reproducing the Introduction paragraph by paragraph. Rebuild the reasoning after major Results changes.

Select evidence for the scientific claim each paragraph explains. Briefly identify the relevant investigation and comparison where applicable, then use decisive results to support interpretation rather than replaying every metric or tool. Apply the general comparison-evidence rule to any result recap. Integrate interpretation-changing mixed or negative outcomes into the corresponding claim, retaining its comparison conditions. An evaluation supports the capability or inference it actually tests; agreement with a reference result does not by itself establish a different, untested capability. Do not refer to “what the Introduction argued” or add unnecessary bold miniheadings when continuous prose is intended.

When a study establishes both a methodological capability and substantive findings about its application domain, distinguish what the evaluation establishes about the method from what the investigations reveal about the domain. Combine the two when their relationship advances the argument, and avoid repeating the same result in adjacent synthesis paragraphs. A methodological, theoretical or resource paper need not include a separate application-domain discovery.

Make each substantive comparison with prior work explain the relationship: does this finding confirm earlier evidence, extend it to a different setting, limit its scope, conflict with it, or explain how previously separate findings relate? Identify the evidence and conditions that justify that relationship. A citation list or another summary of prior studies does not perform this interpretive work; retain background only when it is needed for the comparison.

Discussion may offer a new interpretation or implication, but make the reasoning traceable to established manuscript findings and relevant prior evidence. Do not introduce an unreported empirical result, a new assessment criterion or an unsupported premise as though the study established it. Mark prospective implications at the strength the evidence permits.

When the evidence favours an explanation without uniquely establishing it, consider credible alternatives supported by the design or relevant literature. Explain what discriminates among them and what remains unresolved before treating one explanation as preferred. Do not invent competitors or add a generic caveat to every claim. Keep the established result, supported interpretation and untested implication distinguishable; an unresolved alternative can limit a mechanism claim without invalidating the observed finding.

Before limiting an unfamiliar finding, explain what was observed and what remains unresolved. Describe limitations through the affected claim, actual constraint, interpretive consequence and remaining supported conclusion. For a resource restriction, identify the analysis it prevented or changed and the resulting scope of the evidence. When candidate status or novelty depends on a bounded reference screen, explain the screen's coverage, distinguish an absent match from established novelty or function, and identify the evidence needed to assess the proposed status, function or property. Apply that guidance only when such screening is present. Do not cosmetically cancel a serious limitation with a positive finding or portray a bounded result as worthless. Future work should resolve a named uncertainty rather than advertise larger samples or broader applications generically.

Let limitations explain boundaries and let the closing passage explain the supported scientific consequence. Keep these functions distinguishable whether they occupy separate paragraphs or an author-requested combined passage. Set length by the reasoning needed; a short conclusion does not by itself require merging it with limitations. End with a concrete scientific consequence, not new evidence, an unsupported promise, a component inventory, a slogan or another full Results summary. A separate Conclusion is optional and follows article structure.

**Gate:** the Discussion explains what the findings change through evidence-supported interpretation; the ending follows from that reasoning. Prospective value remains distinct from demonstrated utility.
