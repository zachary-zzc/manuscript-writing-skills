# Paragraph and sentence review

Use these checks at the requested scope under the [shared manuscript contract](contract.md); ordinary prose work does not require loading the full forensic audit.

## Terminology and logical continuity

For a full audit or substantive manuscript revision, apply all three steps across the manuscript. For a bounded edit, examine the affected passage, its neighbouring transitions and necessary dependencies. Reuse the coverage ledger and storyline record; do not create parallel reports. For new drafting, establish terminology and paragraph roles before prose, then audit sentence continuity in the draft. For revision, diagnose the existing argument before rewriting it.

### 1. Terminology table

Maintain one internal terminology table for important research-field and activity labels, entities, analytical units, methods, metrics and study conditions. Include only concepts present in the study.

| Field | Record and check |
|---|---|
| Canonical term | Preferred expression and abbreviation, where needed. |
| Referent and unit | What the term denotes, counts, predicts or evaluates. |
| Definition and scope | Meaning, inclusion criteria and relevant evidence boundary. |
| Role in the argument | Broader context, central contribution, illustrative application or result; check its relationship to the accepted research scope. |
| Variants | Existing expressions and whether they are equivalent, narrower or broader. |
| Distinctions to preserve | Related concepts that must not be treated as interchangeable. |
| Locations | First definition and affected text, figures, embedded artwork, captions, tables and SI. |
| Decision | Retain, define, standardise, distinguish or query, with the reason. |

Audit in both directions: several names may unnecessarily suggest different concepts, while one broad name may collapse different objects, populations, measurements or evidence levels. Do not assume every variant is a synonym. Preserve distinctions between a scientific object, its computational representation and the metric assessing it, where those occur.

For a new or full manuscript, create the table scaffold at H0 and complete its baseline at H1–H4. Use the same canonical terms in Results headings, figure and panel labels, captions, table headers, Methods definitions, SI headings and Abstract/title claims. Update the table's locations and decision status at H5–H9S and H11. Freeze it against the compiled package at H12. Section owners may extend the shared table, but should not create parallel glossaries.

Choose terminology under the [writing guidance](writing-style.md#establish-the-scientific-story). Check that first-use definitions explain necessary unfamiliar concepts before later claims depend on them. Preserve original instruments, quotations and identifiers under the [source-preservation rules](../../manuscript-audit/references/audit.md#scope-evidence-and-findings); add a crosswalk when revised terminology differs from historical wording.

### 2. Paragraph reverse outline

For every paragraph within scope, record:

**Stable ID → purpose → principal claim → evidence or reasoning → relation to the preceding paragraph → contribution to the next step → outcome and repair.**

Examine each paragraph and its boundaries with neighbouring paragraphs:

- Why does it belong here, and what established information makes it understandable?
- Does it advance, support, qualify or interpret the argument? Does it repeat a function without adding a necessary inference?
- Does it introduce a new question, dataset, condition or analytical unit without orientation?
- Does its conclusion follow from its evidence and prepare the next step appropriately?

Flag missing premises, unexplained topic changes, unsupported inferential jumps, repeated introductions and unnecessary backward movement. A finding-first topic sentence is appropriate when the paragraph supplies its supporting evidence. Returning to an earlier topic is acceptable when its purpose is explicit. Check subsection headings against the actual contents and apply the [section gates](writing-quality.md#section-gates); do not invent paragraph counts or compulsory section splits beyond the applicable section requirements and author instructions.

Repair the reasoning through reordering, splitting, merging or supplying a supported connection. A transition word cannot substitute for a missing logical relationship.

When a substantive edit involves repeated claims across headings, paragraphs, captions or sections, compare their functions within the requested scope. Note affected locations and whether each introduces, establishes, interprets, synthesises or bounds the claim in the existing reverse outline or storyline record. A short reminder may be necessary for orientation or a self-contained caption; repeating the full demonstration usually is not. Compress or remove duplication only when no necessary evidence, boundary or reader connection is lost. Do not create a separate repetition ledger or expand a local polish into a whole-paper sweep.

### 3. Sentence continuity

Examine every sentence and adjacent sentence pair within each paragraph in scope, together with paragraph-boundary transitions. Attach sentence-level locations to identified problems rather than treating a paragraph-level pass as evidence that its sentences were checked.

- **Function and necessity:** identify the question, approach, finding, evidence, interpretation or necessary qualification the sentence contributes to the paragraph and overall storyline. Establish the complete reasoning before applying the [necessity filter](writing-style.md#final-necessity-filter) to each sentence, phrase and word, especially in the Introduction, Abstract and Results. Shorten only when clarity and logical continuity are preserved; an explanatory link or purposeful repetition can earn its place without introducing a new claim.
- **Referent:** check the subject and antecedents of expressions such as “this,” “these findings” and “the analysis.” Pronouns are appropriate when immediate and unambiguous.
- **Continuity:** identify the logical relation to the preceding sentence, such as elaboration, evidence, consequence, contrast or a necessary next step. Determine whether a newly introduced scientific object, technology, dataset or method has an explicit role in the argument. Shared vocabulary, repeated topic words or a pronoun do not establish that relation; recover the missing premise rather than supplying a cosmetic transition.
- **Scope concordance:** inspect each word and phrase in the complete sentence, including field labels, nouns, verbs, modifiers and example lists, against the accepted research scope, central contribution and evidence boundary. Apply the [scope guidance](writing-style.md#keep-wording-aligned-with-contribution-scope), distinguishing unintended expansion or narrowing from legitimate background, examples and bounded results. For a mismatch, record the exact expression, implied versus intended scope, supporting evidence and repair in the existing ledger. Check every sentence within the requested review scope, especially in the Introduction and Abstract; sampling keywords or approving a paragraph as a whole is insufficient. Do not require a separate entry for every word that passes.
- **Study conditions:** identify changes in population, analytical unit, metric, comparator, intervention condition or time/version; orient the reader when these affect interpretation.
- **Inference:** test whether the next claim follows from the preceding evidence and whether causal or consequential connectors express a justified relationship. For result summaries and comparisons, apply the [finding-first checks](../../manuscript-results/references/section.md#results-evidence-and-synthesis): identify what was established and its scientific meaning beyond activities or output counts, and verify the evidence for the claimed advantage.
- **Load:** separate distinct claims that need their own explanation, and join fragments that unnecessarily interrupt one argument. Inspect unfamiliar noun stacks, unclear novel compounds or verbs, deeply embedded clauses and long gaps between actor, action and object. Apply the [direct-prose guidance](writing-style.md#write-direct-academic-prose) to make the relationships explicit; preserve necessary technical terms, useful connectors and repeated referents. Judge the burden in context, without fixed sentence-length or word-count thresholds.
- **Reader paraphrase:** check whether an intended reader can restate the claim, identify who or what acts on which object under which conditions, and explain its relation to the preceding claim without guessing a missing link. Where an actor/action frame does not apply, identify the stated relation directly. Use this as an editorial check, not evidence of a human comprehension test. If the paraphrase needs a fact absent from the text and its context, restore a supported explanation or flag the unresolved fact.

After repairing or compressing a passage, repeat the necessity, scope-concordance, sentence-pair, reader-paraphrase and paragraph-boundary checks on the actual replacement, including its first and last sentences in context. Compare the revised meaning with the original accepted meaning and the supporting evidence: preserve claims, conditions, comparisons, uncertainty and scientific distinctions unless an authorized correction changes them. Do not mark a passage coherent solely because its terminology is consistent or its prose is fluent.

Do not let adjacency imply that one experiment produced another experiment's result. State documented analytical dependencies; otherwise identify the change of study or condition. Check that unfamiliar terms, acronyms and implementation details do not interrupt the scientific reasoning. Apply the [qualification rules](writing-style.md#remove-defensive-writing-without-hiding-boundaries) so necessary boundaries remain with their claims without repetitive defensive endings.
