# Editorial guideline: release narrative and defensive-writing

> **GUIDELINE STATUS: MANDATORY EDITORIAL DEFAULT**
> Apply this guideline across manuscript writing, storyline, idea framing, innovation claims, polishing, figures and final review.

These are mandatory defaults for manuscript writing, idea and innovation framing, restructuring, polishing, figure and caption narrative, and final review. A manuscript is a public launch of its strongest supported contribution, not a work report.

## Hard rules

1. **Organize around the strongest supported advantage.** Build the narrative around the clearest contribution and its strongest evidence. Do not let chronology, an activity list, or the volume of work define the story.
2. **Do not write a process report.** Do not organize the manuscript around what was done, tried, changed, or failed. Retain process detail only when it is needed for reproducibility or interpretation.
3. **Remove self-undermining language.** Treat phrases such as “unfortunately,” “only,” “still markedly behind,” and “the effect was limited” as inspection flags. Do not volunteer a reviewer-facing summary of weaknesses. When a limitation changes interpretation, state the exact evidence boundary, condition, design choice, or unresolved claim neutrally and where it matters.
4. **Do not make an unwinnable metric the contest.** Use a justified evaluation dimension, operating regime, task, or comparison basis that matches the contribution. Do not cherry-pick, redefine metrics, or omit required comparisons.
5. **Do not call a non-leading result a loss.** Explain it through objective differences, operating conditions, or a supported trade-off while preserving the factual comparison and its scope.
6. **State advantages explicitly.** Name each supported advantage in the prose and connect it to the evidence. Do not rely on a table or figure to make the reader discover the contribution.
7. **Give every experiment an argumentative duty.** Every experiment, analysis, display, and subsection must advance a defined claim, comparison, mechanism, boundary, or reproducibility requirement. Delete, merge, or relocate material with no duty.
8. **Open like a launch.** The Abstract and Introduction should move through problem → gap → idea or innovation → hardest result. Lead with the scientific need and the contribution, then show the strongest supported outcome.
9. **Make the conclusion reinforce the memory.** Strengthen the central contribution and supported payoff. Do not introduce a sudden self-negation in the conclusion.

## Mandatory defensive-writing audit

Whenever the user supplies manuscript text, inspect all defensive writing and report every instance. For each instance, provide:

- the exact phrase and location;
- the hard rule it violates;
- why it weakens the scientific presentation or turns the paper into a process report; and
- a concrete, evidence-bounded replacement.

Distinguish rhetorical self-weakening from a material scientific boundary. Never delete or conceal a required limitation, negative or null finding, uncertainty, ethics statement, safety statement, or reproducibility disclosure. Recast those items as precise scope, condition, evidence, or trade-off statements, and keep them where they are needed for correct interpretation. These rules sharpen the presentation; they do not permit overclaiming, cherry-picking, or misreporting.
