# Personal manuscript writing quality

Apply these rules at the requested scale, in every interaction mode and language. The confirmed mode governs human checkpoints. A paragraph edit does not require rebuilding the paper. Reuse the accepted storyline, terminology, factual basis and author decisions. Current, scoped author instructions and verified target requirements take precedence over defaults.

Read and apply the **Editorial Guideline (mandatory default)** in [release narrative and defensive-writing](release-narrative-rules.md) before drafting, revising or polishing.

## Establish the scientific story

Build the argument from the research question, a real unresolved problem, the demonstrated contribution, ordered findings and integrated implication. Distinguish a methodological advance, an empirical finding, a theoretical result and a resource contribution. An architecture diagram, activity list or large dataset does not by itself establish scientific utility.

Maintain a compact problem–contribution–evidence map. Each Results subsection must advance that argument through a distinct finding, necessary support or material boundary, with its question and role clear. Order findings by scientific dependency and importance. Do not let software components, the chronology of experiments or available plots dictate the narrative.

Exercise scientific judgment rather than merely polishing the author's suggested interpretation. Treat author-suggested explanations and earlier assistant drafts as hypotheses to assess, not as evidence. Verify substantive empirical and technical assertions before presenting them, including background concepts, resource descriptions, mechanisms and motivating drawbacks; place primary-source citations at the claims they support. Distinguish reported findings from inferences based on them, and qualify or omit unsupported assertions. Check counterexamples, the relevant scope or version, and existing mitigations before generalising a limitation. Attribute existing resources, tools and released models accurately. Integration can be an advance without implying that every ingredient is new.

Maintain the [terminology table](paragraph-review.md#1-terminology-table) for important concepts, with the scope appropriate to the task. Prefer established, precise terms; introduce a new term only when it names a necessary distinction, and explain its scientific purpose and defining properties at first use. Do not select labels that imply unverified capabilities or vary technical terms for lexical variety. Keep actual actors, methods and evaluated objects distinct; researcher, model, agent, expert, evaluator and execution environment are relevant only where present. Prefer full wording for short terms, especially two-word phrases; write “knowledge graph” in full rather than introducing “KG.” Introduce other abbreviations only when they materially improve readability and are familiar to the intended audience or defined at first use. Preserve conventional scientific abbreviations, exact names, quotations and formal notation where expansion would be inappropriate.

Identify the role of a cited study or method, preserving actual bibliographic citations rather than unexplained reference-number shorthand or drafting placeholders. Preserve the distinction between analysis, interpretation and presentation.

## Keep wording aligned with contribution scope

Check every word, phrase and sentence for the scope it conveys, especially in the Introduction and Abstract. Align research-field labels, activities, objectives and claims with the accepted central idea, actual contribution and supporting evidence. Distinguish the broader motivating context, the intended contribution, illustrative applications and conclusions established by particular results. Prevent wording from expanding a contribution into a broader field or reducing it to a few examples. A demonstration does not establish unrestricted generality, and an example does not define the full contribution unless the study is explicitly limited to it.

Broader background and narrower examples or result statements are appropriate when their roles and relationship to the central argument are clear. Mark examples as illustrative when needed; retain narrower evidence boundaries for individual results. Do not repair accidental narrowing by making an unsupported capability claim. Scope concordance concerns meaning, not identical wording in every sentence. Apply the [sentence-level scope audit](paragraph-review.md#3-sentence-continuity) before stylistic polishing and repeat it on the final replacement; fluent phrasing and consistent terminology alone do not establish concordance.

## Write direct academic prose

Make the subject, action, object and comparison understandable on first reading. Prefer familiar, precise words to dense nominal phrases or rare vocabulary. Give each sentence one principal point and each paragraph one scientific job. Develop the argument through evidence, comparison, explanation or qualification, avoiding both overloaded sentences and fragmented sequences of tiny paragraphs. Apply the [paragraph and sentence continuity checks](paragraph-review.md#2-paragraph-reverse-outline) to expose missing premises, ambiguous referents and silent changes of scope before polishing transitions.

Unpack unfamiliar noun stacks and newly coined compounds when their relationships are unclear. State what modifies, measures or acts on what. Keep the actor, action and object close enough to follow without holding several intervening clauses in memory; move or split deeply embedded clauses where that helps. Prefer a familiar literal verb to an unclear metaphor or a verb newly formed from a noun. Preserve established terminology, exact formal names, quotations, necessary technical detail and uncertainty; explain an unfamiliar term rather than replacing a precise distinction with a vague word.

Retain words that make the reasoning and syntax explicit. In English, words such as “that,” “which” and “because,” and repetition of an established referent, can make a passage easier to follow when omission would obscure the relationship. Use them where justified by the meaning, not as a formula; apply the equivalent judgment in other languages. These are contextual editing choices, not universal word bans or fixed sentence-length limits. Check understanding through the [reader paraphrase check](paragraph-review.md#3-sentence-continuity).

Source note: [arXiv:2610.29162v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29162v1) examines linguistic proxies and a small proof-of-concept length-penalty experiment; it does not directly test human comprehension or prompting interventions. The guidance here is an editorial adaptation, not a validated treatment. Evaluate each revision against its meaning and evidence, not a proxy score alone.

Do not use a statement-then-colon pattern in manuscript narrative, whether the lead-in is a label, a complete assertion or a generic interpretation (for example, “Genus-specific evaluation revealed substantial differences: F1 was ...”). State results directly in complete sentences and integrate necessary interpretation without a colon-led restatement. Avoid habitual em dashes, colon splices and stacked punctuation. Preserve punctuation that belongs to exact titles, identifiers, ratios, notation, quotations or structured fields.

Do not use semicolons in newly authored manuscript narrative, captions or explanatory Supplementary Information prose. Split independent claims into sentences, or express their logical relation with a suitable conjunction. Preserve punctuation in verbatim quotations, original instruments, code, identifiers and formal notation.

Avoid repeated AI transitions such as “Together, these results,” “Taken together,” “first…next…finally” and identical “To test…” openings. Avoid a sequence of identically phrased comparisons, including repeated “compared with”; combine related comparisons or vary sentence structure while keeping each comparator, metric and direction explicit. State the scientific aim naturally. Do not replace one repeated template with another.

Apply author comments about scenario, emphasis and structure to the scientific narrative without reproducing drafting instructions or conversational framing in the manuscript. Describe concrete actors, scientific operations and objects. Make comparative and additive expressions explicit: identify what was added and relative to which analysis or condition.

When presenting a substantive scientific result or recapping an evaluated comparison, lead with the strongest supported conclusion and explain what the decisive observations or numbers mean for that conclusion. Name the assessed object, relevant comparison and scope; place interpretation beside its supporting evidence rather than leaving the reader to infer meaning from a list of values. Match the conclusion to the measurement: association or consistency alone does not establish agreement, accuracy, reliability or causation. Do not force an additional interpretation onto every descriptive number. Do not substitute evaluative words such as “greater” or “deeper” for evidence. Select support appropriate to the study; qualitative and theoretical findings do not require invented numerical scores.

Use operationally defined outcomes rather than unqualified evaluative labels such as “satisfactory,” “good” or “high quality.” For assessed outcomes, identify the evaluator, what was scored and the decision rule or scale needed to interpret the result. Do not replace a recorded endpoint with vague “quality criteria,” or relabel a composite pass rate as accuracy. Preserve explicitly defined subjective measures and exact rubric labels, attributing their judgements to the assessor. State the minimum necessary definition near the result and place detailed scoring procedures in Methods or supporting material.

In newly written manuscript prose, avoid the author's rejected wording “using,” “scientific reporting,” “backbone,” “copies” and vague “per system.” Rewrite the actual operation, model, object or condition. Inspect context instead of global string replacement. Preserve faithful quotations, technical names, code, instrument wording and indispensable mathematical meanings.

Avoid “connect” as a generic relational verb in manuscript prose; describe the specific relation or operation. Preserve exact quotations and necessary technical terms.

Prefer “average” in ordinary prose when accurate. Preserve arithmetic versus geometric means, weighting, macro averaging and the distinction from a median. State the estimator where interpretation depends on it. Name the actual analytical work instead of “downstream analyses.” Prefer data, analytical outputs, reports or figures to storage terminology when storage is irrelevant.

| Weak wording | More precise direction |
|---|---|
| “Using the backbone, copies were scored per system.” | “The evaluator scored each model's generated reports.” Verify that these are the actual objects and actors. |
| “This enabled better scientific reporting.” | Name the improvement, such as more accurate report interpretations or clearer visual comparisons. |
| “The average improved.” | Name the metric, aggregation and comparison; retain the exact estimator where necessary. |

## Remove defensive writing without hiding boundaries

Apply the mandatory [release narrative and defensive-writing rules](release-narrative-rules.md) in addition to the local checks below.

State supported findings confidently and proportionately. Avoid unsolicited declarations about what the authors are not claiming, not attempting or not intending. Do not add generic warnings merely because the work involves AI, prediction or computation. Every stated constraint needs a verified design choice, missing measurement, observed failure or specific uncertainty.

For each qualification, ask four questions.

1. What source establishes it?
2. Which exact claim becomes misleading if it disappears?
3. Does it add a distinct boundary or repeat one already stated?
4. Does the reader need it here, or would Methods, SI or one Discussion passage suffice?

Delete unsupported or redundant caveats. Place interpretation-changing boundaries beside the affected claim or metric. Keep procedural detail in Methods, SI or Discussion when those locations preserve the necessary interpretation. A Results paragraph should normally conclude with its supported scientific meaning rather than an unrelated validation caveat; retain a qualification as the conclusion when the boundary itself is the finding. Preserve material negative findings and discrepancies. Keep required reproducibility, ethics and reporting information in an appropriate required location. Detailed rebuttal reasoning belongs in SI or correspondence unless essential to understanding the result. Do not explain an explanation repeatedly.

Treat “only,” “merely,” “rather than,” “cannot,” “does not establish” and defensive contrasts as inspection flags, not automatic errors. Retain a distinction when it prevents a real misinterpretation. Choose “shows” or “demonstrates” for direct evidence, “supports” or “suggests” for a justified but indirect inference, and “may” or “could” for an unverified possibility. One calibrated verb is normally enough. Avoid hedge stacks such as “may perhaps suggest.”

Check modal meaning during revision and translation in context. In a claim about what may be true, “may not” can express possible non-occurrence, whereas “cannot” makes a stronger exclusion or inability claim. In a permissions statement, “may not” or “cannot” can instead prohibit an action; preserve that restriction. “Must not” normally prohibits something, whereas “need not” removes a requirement. Preserve the intended sense instead of treating these as interchangeable hedges. For scientific necessity, exclusion or causal claims, identify the logical or study-design basis that licenses the wording; otherwise narrow the claim. Distinguish a recommendation for future studies from an author's commitment to perform them. Do not turn “should be tested” into “we will test” without an established commitment.

| Defensive or inflated | Better scientific treatment |
|---|---|
| “We do not claim definitive general reliability.” | State the evaluated tasks and conditions directly. If reliability across runs is claimed, report the relevant replication evidence or its actual absence. |
| “Further validation is needed.” | Name the unresolved claim and the discriminating observation or experiment. |
| “The analysis identifies the receptor.” | If evidence is predictive or associative, describe a receptor candidate or supported hypothesis and the evidence supporting that status. |

Do not relabel every promising scientific hypothesis as a weakness. Distinguish the evidence, the supported interpretation and the established conclusion at the strength appropriate to the discipline. Preserve the useful scientific inference at its demonstrated strength.

## References

For substantive literature searches or citation verification, read [literature.md](literature.md) at the requested scope. Ordinary wording edits retain the relevant existing citations and need only the checks affected by the edit.

For a full research article, search and map the relevant literature across the foundational concepts, closest competing studies, empirical context, methods and data provenance. Integrate sources at the claims they support; a short bibliography that omits major strands is not adequate coverage. Honour author- or journal-specified reference requirements with relevant, distinct sources actually cited in the text. A numerical request is not permission to add unrelated papers, citation clusters without a scientific purpose, duplicate working-paper and journal versions, or uncited bibliography entries.

Verify references against the exact claim, strength and source actually read. Prefer primary sources for technical capability and novelty. Check bibliographic identity, publication status and any correction or retraction signal; distinguish an abstract from an inspected full text and a working draft from its published version. Retain method credit during compression and sufficient relevant current coverage. Do not pad to historical citation totals or invent bibliographic details.

## Final necessity filter

Scientific accuracy, a clear storyline and complete reasoning take priority over concision. Establish each paragraph's argument, the links between its sentences and its role in the whole manuscript before shortening it. Within the requested review scope, require every paragraph, sentence, phrase and word to serve that reasoning; this is a test of contribution, not a requirement to minimise length. Apply it especially to the Introduction, Abstract and Results. Accuracy, topical relevance, fluent wording or an accompanying citation alone does not justify inclusion.

Aim for concise explanation, not minimum length. Fewer words, shorter sentences and familiar vocabulary do not by themselves establish comprehension.

During drafting and final review, apply a deletion or compression test: what necessary meaning, evidence, logical connection or precision would be lost if this expression disappeared? Delete or merge only when the argument remains equally clear and adequately supported; relocate material needed elsewhere. Retain explanatory steps, explicit transitions and purposeful repetition of established terms when they help the reader follow the reasoning. Remove repeated premises, generic mechanism restatements, method name-dropping and transitions only when they add no scientific or connective value. Before adding material, check whether existing text already fulfils its function. Check expressions in context; a separate ledger entry for every passing word is unnecessary.

Allocate any supplied or verified word budget according to scientific importance; do not invent section quotas. Preserve essential premises, conclusion-changing evidence, method credit, citations and qualifications that determine interpretation, including negative findings. Retain words needed for grammatical clarity and coherent transitions. Compression must not create missing premises, telegraphic fragments or overloaded sentences. Re-read the revised paragraph and its boundaries against the earlier accepted argument. If shortening weakens the storyline, obscures a logical step or makes the reader reconstruct omitted reasoning, restore or rewrite the needed explanation rather than prefer the shorter version.

After revision, recheck affected terms, numbers, claims, Methods, legends and SI; expand only when new discrepancies warrant it. Keep unsupported, unassessed and verified states distinct. Compilation, numerical reproduction, scientific support, visual quality and venue compliance are separate achievements.
