# Literature search and citation verification

Use for substantive literature discovery, claim support, reference correction or citation export. A local grammar edit needs only the affected citation checks. Reuse the existing claim/evidence record; do not require a separate literature database for a small task. These procedures extend the [shared reference rules](writing-style.md#references).

## Search the scientific question

Translate the claim into entities, relationship, outcome, context and boundary. Search precise terms, accepted synonyms and a broader formulation when needed; use controlled vocabulary such as MeSH when relevant to the database. Look for contrary findings and alternative explanations as well as agreement. Record databases, query/date and important inclusion limits when a search informs a novelty or coverage claim. An unsuccessful query means no result was found by that search, not that no relevant study exists.

Choose sources for relevance and evidential value. Do not restrict the default search to prestigious journal families, citation counts, language or familiar institutions. Honour an explicit search restriction, but report the resulting coverage limit rather than turn it into an unrestricted novelty claim. A targeted search does not become a systematic review merely because several databases were consulted.

Deduplicate by verified identifiers and bibliographic identity, then inspect ambiguous title/author matches. Link preprints, corrections and final publications without counting them as independent evidence; preserve the version actually inspected. Do not merge distinct studies merely because titles or authors resemble one another. Keep foundational work when recent-date filtering would otherwise remove it.

## Judge support separately from identity

For each consequential claim, retain its source, inspected location/version and a short reason for the support assessment. Use these categories when they clarify a disputed or uncertain citation:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct support | The inspected evidence supports the stated relationship within the relevant design and conditions. |
| Partial support | It supports a narrower condition or one component; split or narrow the claim as needed. |
| Background | It supplies context, not evidence for this specific result or mechanism. |
| Contradictory or limiting | It conflicts with, or restricts, the claim and must be considered in interpretation. |
| Candidate only | Discovery metadata suggests relevance; substantive support has not been inspected. |

Distinguish title/metadata, abstract and full-text inspection. Read the necessary methods, results, figure or supplement before claiming that it supports details unavailable in the abstract. A DOI that resolves correctly verifies identity, not claim support. Reviews can orient the search; use the original study for an experimental claim where available. Put each citation beside the clause it supports, and do not let a citation to one clause appear to establish a stronger neighbouring claim.

## Reconcile bibliographic records

Compare title, complete author order, publication/version, year, journal, volume, issue, pages or article number, and DOI against publisher or authoritative registry records. Distinguish online and issue dates, article numbers and pages, and substantive identity errors from formatting variants. Do not infer a publication year from digits inside a DOI. Record disputed fields, current and proposed values, source and unresolved status before correcting them.

A missing Crossref record or failed request is not proof of an invalid DOI. Check the DOI resolver, relevant registry and publisher; distinguish service failure, unavailable metadata and a confirmed mismatched work. Do not use arbitrary page-number differences to assign scientific severity. Check correction/retraction status and preserve consequential notices.

When exporting BibTeX/RIS or another requested format, preserve structured author names/order and corporate authors; do not reconstruct a full list from an abbreviated display citation. Leave unknown fields explicit or absent rather than invent them. Inspect the resulting records and applicable import syntax before claiming readiness. Produce the requested local correction/export; external reference-library writes require their own authorization.
