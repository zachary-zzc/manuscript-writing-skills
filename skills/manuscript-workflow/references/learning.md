# Approval-based learning from feedback

## Assess feedback in every language

In Normal and Expert, assess every new substantive user comment, correction or requirement for a reusable rule throughout discovery, storyline, writing, visualization, audit and revision. Interpret its underlying scientific, rhetorical or workflow meaning regardless of feedback/manuscript language. Preserve meaningful original wording and context in the working record; formulate proposals in the conversation's current language. A language switch changes neither mode nor this obligation.

Apply feedback locally within its scope and existing authorization, unless discussion or preview was requested. Manuscript changes and persistent learning are separate: accepting prose does not approve a skill update. Even “from now on, all abstracts…” requires a concrete save question in Normal/Expert. “Update the skill's abstract rule with this exception” directly authorizes that specific update; do not ask identical permission again.

## Audit generalizability

Before proposing persistence, determine:

- **Meaning:** will it help comparable future manuscript work, or does it merely describe this result?
- **Scope:** preserve section, figure type, language, project, temporary condition, trigger and exceptions.
- **Authority:** distinguish the human's instruction from a question, quoted source, third-party view or unaccepted assistant suggestion.
- **Integrity:** do not invent facts, suppress negative evidence, erase necessary boundaries or impose unsupported constraints through generalization.
- **Coverage:** identify existing guidance, duplication, refinement, conditional exception or conflict.
- **Destination:** general instruction, relevant reference, project profile or current task only.

Do not promote numerical findings, model/cohort identities, study conclusions, fixed panel/paragraph counts, one-off placement or temporary resource/journal constraints into universal defaults. A conditional preference can be reusable when its conditions remain explicit.

## Propose, ask, then save

For a worthwhile candidate provide the feedback/context, exact proposed rule, scope/trigger/exceptions, target skill/reference section, add/refine/replace operation and conflicts. Add a short example when useful. Ask explicitly whether to **approve**, **revise**, or **keep task-specific**.

For example: “Save this Abstract rule: ‘Omit detailed validation procedures unless essential to the headline contribution’; revise it, or keep it specific to this manuscript?”

Bundle related candidates at a natural review point without burying them in a change log or interrupting every minor edit. Continue independently authorized manuscript work while awaiting a decision. No response is not approval. A declined rule stays local; do not persist an equivalent paraphrase elsewhere.

After approval, change only the agreed instruction/reference, preserve unrelated rules and record the decision in the existing working record. Inspect the exact diff for scope, duplicates and conflicts; verify links and skill validation, with a small behavioral check for a substantive change when warranted. Report the saved rule and destination concisely.

## Easy and mode changes

In Easy, offer no proactive skill-upgrade questions during work or final delivery and make no silent persistent changes. Follow an explicit request to update a particular rule within its stated scope. Switching into Easy suspends unanswered proposals without approving, rejecting or saving them; they do not block manuscript execution. Returning to Normal/Expert permits assessment/proposals again, but neither old feedback nor mode changes retroactively authorize persistence.
