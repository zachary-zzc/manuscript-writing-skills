# Statistical reporting and inference

Use when drafting, revising or auditing statistical analyses or inferential claims. A descriptive sample count does not activate a complete statistics review. Apply these checks to the requested passage and its necessary dependencies; keep findings in the existing record and preserve the confirmed writing mode. The [numerical, statistical and comparative checks](audit.md#numerical-statistical-and-comparative-checks) own experimental units, dependence, multiplicity families, interval definitions and comparison scope.

## Match the report to the actual analysis

For each major analysis in scope, identify the endpoint, comparison and test or model from the retained study records. Check that the Methods explain the relevant specification, transformations, assumptions and software/version when known. State randomization, blinding and missing-data handling only when applicable to the actual design and supported by records; do not infer them from standard practice.

Report an effect estimate and relevant uncertainty when the analysis provides them, so readers can assess magnitude as well as statistical evidence. When P values are used, preserve significant and non-significant results and prefer the available exact values over stars or thresholds alone. If only a threshold or rounded value is retained, report that limit rather than inventing precision. Missing estimates or intervals are reporting or evidence gaps to assess, not permission to calculate new ones automatically.

Check test direction, test statistic, degrees of freedom, repeat counts and panel-specific sample sizes where needed to interpret the actual analysis or required by the verified target instructions. These fields are not all applicable to every model or paper. Keep journal-specific requirements conditional on that journal and submission stage; do not attribute a local preference to the journal.

## Check the inference and its assumptions

A significant effect in one group and a non-significant effect in another do not establish a difference between the effects. Look for a direct effect comparison or an appropriate interaction analysis before accepting that claim. If the retained evidence cannot support it, flag the gap or narrow the wording; do not silently launch a new analysis.

Check the stated basis for assumptions that affect interpretation, such as independence, distribution or variance assumptions. A small sample or an unsupported assertion of normality does not establish those assumptions. Inspect the diagnostics or methodological rationale actually supplied; distinguish an unreported check from a demonstrated violation. Do not prescribe a replacement test before the design and unit of analysis are clear.

## Make exclusions traceable

For exclusions that affect the result, record the criterion, when it was set, how many observations or units were removed and from which groups. Distinguish a prespecified rule from a decision made after inspecting outcomes. Describe post-hoc decisions honestly rather than rewriting them as planned.

Inspect any retained analysis of whether including the excluded observations changes the conclusion. If that evidence is absent and matters to the claim, record the uncertainty and possible resolution; do not claim robustness or perform new reanalysis without authorization. Preserve the original records and the existing rules for propagating authorized corrections.
