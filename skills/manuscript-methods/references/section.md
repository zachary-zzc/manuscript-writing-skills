### Methods

Describe the scientific procedure sufficiently to understand and reconstruct central results. Organise procedures relevant to the actual study into meaningful peer-level topical subsections; include architecture and evaluation setup where applicable. Avoid deep numbered sub-subsection trees. Describe shared procedures once, then explain genuine differences between studies.

Describe the study's inputs or premises, operations or reasoning, outputs and scientific purpose. Specify the relevant procedures and assumptions for the actual study design. Data provenance, eligibility, exclusions, cohort/split construction, model versions, historical settings, comparison conditions, prompts/rubrics, metric definitions, aggregation, inferential units and statistical procedures apply only when those objects or methods are present; they are not a checklist requiring every manuscript to contain them. Explain consequential choices. Include equations for necessary definitions, with symbols and combinations explained; omit trivial bookkeeping equations.

Separate tested historical behaviour from later software capability. Current defaults do not prove earlier run settings. Explain genuine resource constraints, substitutions or incomplete runs where they affect the result. Avoid turning the article into an API, filesystem, SQL-table or retry/status manual. Preserve necessary operational details through appropriate reproducibility material.

**Gate:** every central result has a clear Methods home. Relevant quantities and procedures can be reconstructed; an unexplained code link does not substitute for scientific description.
